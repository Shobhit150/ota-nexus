Setting a room rate can seem straightforward. Look at nearby hotels, choose a competitive price, and update it when business gets busy.

In practice, effective hotel pricing requires more thought. Demand changes, different guests book at different times, and the best rate depends on both the market and the property's commercial goals.

## Understand your demand patterns

Start by reviewing your property's booking history.

Look for differences between weekdays and weekends, high and low seasons, local events, holidays, and booking lead times. These patterns can help you understand when demand tends to strengthen or soften.

Historical performance is useful, but it should not be treated as a guarantee. Current booking pace and market conditions matter too.

## Know your competitive position

Competitor rates provide context, not a price you must copy.

Compare properties with similar locations, facilities, room types, guest experiences, and target audiences. A hotel with a different offering may reasonably charge a different rate.

Review your positioning alongside the rates guests can actually book, including applicable restrictions and offers.

## Review more than occupancy

High occupancy can look encouraging, but it does not tell the whole story.

Average daily rate (ADR) measures the average room revenue earned per occupied room. Revenue per available room (RevPAR) combines room revenue and available room capacity.

These measures provide different perspectives. Looking at them together can help reveal whether occupancy growth is accompanied by stronger room revenue.

It is also important to consider distribution costs and other operating expenses when assessing overall commercial performance.

## Use promotions with a purpose

Discounts can help stimulate demand, but frequent price reductions can weaken positioning or reduce revenue without creating enough additional bookings.

Before launching a promotion, decide what it is intended to achieve. Is the goal to generate demand on slower dates, attract a particular market, encourage longer stays, or improve conversion?

Afterward, review the results against that objective.

## Build a regular review habit

Pricing decisions benefit from a consistent routine. Review booking pace, upcoming demand, competitor positioning, cancellations, and revenue performance on a schedule that suits your property.

Make adjustments based on what the information shows, rather than reacting to every competitor price change.

## The takeaway

Good revenue management is not simply about charging more. It is about making informed pricing decisions that reflect demand, competition, property positioning, and business objectives.

A thoughtful pricing strategy gives hotel operators a clearer basis for deciding when to hold rates, when to adjust them, and when a promotion makes sense.
